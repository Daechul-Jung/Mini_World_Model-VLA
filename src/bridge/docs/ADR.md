# Architecture Decision Records -- Bridge

*Private. Gitignored.*

---

### ADR-B01: The bridge is a third package; neither project imports the other

**Decision.** `src/vla/` and `src/world_model/` both import `src/common/` and
nothing of each other. `src/bridge/` is the only place that knows about both.

**Reason.** The two tracks have to stay independently runnable and independently
debuggable. If the VLA imported the world model, a broken dynamics model would
break VLA training, and "which model is wrong" would stop being answerable.

**Trade-off.** Shared structures (`Observation`, `Action`, `ActionSpec`) have to
live in `common/`, which means changing them touches both projects. That is the
correct cost -- it makes the seam explicit.

### ADR-B02: Prefer robot-conditioned dynamics over action translation

**Decision.** For the robotics half, train stage C with `action_kind="robot"` on
action-labelled OpenX episodes. The `LearnedActionProjector` exists but is the
fallback.

**Reason.** Translating a continuous 7-DoF action into one of 8 unsupervised
latent codes is lossy by construction -- 8 codes cannot express a continuous
action space, and the codes mean whatever the video data made them mean. If the
data has real action labels, conditioning on them directly removes the problem
rather than approximating around it.

**Trade-off.** Requires action-labelled video, so it cannot use LSUN or
unlabelled room footage. Those keep the latent-action path. The interesting
unexplored option is one model doing both with modality dropout --
`research/011_unified_action_conditioning.md`.

### ADR-B03: Every reward must report uncertainty

**Decision.** `RewardModel.forward` returns `(reward, info)` and `info` must
contain `uncertainty`.

**Reason.** An RL policy optimising a learned reward will drive it far outside
its training distribution -- that is what optimisation does. A reward that cannot
say "I do not know about this input" gives a confident wrong number exactly where
it matters most. Ensemble disagreement is the cheap standard estimate; a single
model can use a density proxy.

**Trade-off.** Every new reward has to produce a number it may not have a
principled way to compute. A crude proxy that is logged beats no signal at all.

### ADR-B04: In-dream metrics are labelled and never reported as results

**Decision.** `BaseEnv.is_imagined` exists and must be logged next to any
success rate. `configs/bridge/rl_in_dream.yaml` has a mandatory `validate_env`.

**Reason.** A success rate measured inside a learned world model and one measured
in a simulator are different quantities. Conflating them is the easiest way to
produce a result that evaporates, and the failure mode is silent -- the in-dream
number can improve monotonically while the real one does not move.

**Trade-off.** Simulator validation is slow, so it runs every N iterations rather
than every one. That is a sampling-rate decision, not a decision to skip it.

---

### ADR-B05: On-policy RL by default

**Decision.** PPO/GRPO are the target algorithms; off-policy methods with large
replay buffers are not.

**Reason.** Rollouts inside a world model are cheap, so on-policy's sample
inefficiency costs little. What off-policy buys -- reuse of old experience -- is
actively harmful here, because that experience was generated by a *learned*
model and its errors accumulate in the buffer rather than being regenerated.

**Trade-off.** More world-model forward passes per update. The right trade when
the environment is a neural network rather than a physics engine.

---

### ADR-B06: KL to the BC policy is not optional

**Decision.** `RLAlgorithm` takes a `reference_policy` and a `kl_coef`, and the
default config sets `kl_coef: 0.05`.

**Reason.** With two learned models to exploit and no ground truth, the BC policy
is the only anchor to behaviour known to be physically real. Without the penalty,
divergence is not a risk -- it is the expected outcome.

**Trade-off.** Caps how far RL can improve on BC. Correct given the setup;
loosen it only once simulator validation shows the world model is trustworthy
over the rollout horizon being used.
